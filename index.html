How it works

  1. Layers. Assign each layer to the macro step, the micro step, or leave it off. Any combination works; with no micro layers, all chosen layers are clustered together in one step.
  2. Standardize. Each layer is converted to z-scores over Kansas, so all layers weigh the same.
  3. Macrozones. k-means on the macro layers.
  4. Microzones (optional). k-means on the micro layers within each macrozone, so every microzone sits inside one macrozone.
  5. Clean small patches (optional). A 3 × 3 or 5 × 5 km majority filter removes isolated pixels.
  6. Masks (optional). Keep only one soil texture class, or the counties where a crop is grown. The zones do not change.

Number of zones

The silhouette score (−1 to 1) measures how well separated the zones are; auto picks the highest. Kansas climate is a smooth gradient, so macro scores are often nearly flat, and the number of zones is then a choice of detail.

Masks

Masked areas are greyed out and left out of the table and downloads. Both masks can be used together.

Soil texture: the USDA texture class of each pixel, from its 0–60 cm sand and clay.

Crop: counties are ranked by crop area, and the largest are kept until together they hold the chosen share of the Kansas crop area. The crop % column assumes the crop is spread evenly within each county. Crop data: see Layers.

Zone codes and colours

Codes are macrozone.microzone (for example 2.1), numbered from low to high by the first layer of each step. Each macrozone has its own hue, and its microzones are shades of it.

Downloads

CSV: area and layer means of each zone, with the settings used. GeoTIFF: 1-km grid, EPSG:4326, value = macrozone × 10 + microzone, 0 = no data.

About

Groups Kansas into environments (agro-ecological zones) for choosing trial and breeding sites, scaling crop-model runs, or placing monitoring stations. Everything runs in your browser on a 1-km grid; nothing is uploaded.
